    How it works · Step 1 of 5

    It all starts with every touch of every match

    The raw data is the official NCAA play-by-play from . Every serve, pass, set, attack, dig and block is logged along with the player and the score.

    Here's one real rally from the 2025 final

    Each card is one possession, and a new possession starts every time the ball goes over the net.

    What needed cleaning up and what's missing

    • Rallies are rebuilt from changes in the score. The raw data also credits errors to the wrong team, and about 1,700 matches in the 2025 files show up twice, so both of those get fixed.
    • The data doesn't record how good each touch was, where the ball went, block touches that stay in play, or lineups. That means the model only sees teams, not individual players.
    Step 2 of 5

    Every team gets twelve numbers

    Each part of a rally gets two numbers: serving, passing, the first attack after the pass, stopping the other team's first attack, and the long rallies on both offense and defense. Here's how one of Kentucky's twelve gets made.

      The 12 numbers behind

      Counted is what happened in the team's own matches. Adjusted is the model's estimate against an average opponent, and bold numbers beat the league average. This shows Team A from the Match simulator.

      Step 3 of 5

      Turning two teams into a win probability

      Nothing gets simulated here. The model adds up the probability of every possible rally and final score exactly. This uses the matchup from the Match simulator.

      Good nights and bad nights

      Those twelve numbers describe a team's average, but nobody plays exactly to their average every night. The model allows for that by averaging the odds over good, average and bad nights.

      The rally model beats XGBoost

      XGBoost is the go-to machine learning model for tables of stats. This version got season-to-date numbers for both teams (hitting, aces, blocks, record, last season) and the same week-by-week rules.

      Lower log loss means better odds. The rally model won the tournament test in all seasons. The gap is widest early in the season, when XGBoost has only a few matches of stats to go on and the rally model leans on last season. The rally model also adjusts every number for who the opponent was, which matters most in December when everyone left is good.

      What the model doesn't know

      • Injuries, lineup changes and transfers. A team missing its best hitter just looks like a team having a bad stretch.
      • How good each touch was. A perfect pass and a shanked one count the same.
      • Hot streaks that last a whole tournament. Each match gets its own good or bad night, so a team that catches fire in December is underrated. Three straight champions started below 2%, which the model put at about 1 in 9,000.
      • How teams from different years compare. Cross-season matchups assume the overall level of play stays about the same.
      Step 5 of 5

      Ask the model, and check its work

      The volleyball button in the corner opens a chat with an AI assistant. It doesn't answer from memory. It looks things up with the same model and math that run this site, and every number it gives you gets checked before you see it.

      1. It looks it up

        The assistant picks from eleven tools: match odds for any two teams and seasons, odds from any score, team profiles, rankings, bracket odds, tournament results, what decided an upset, and read-only queries over the box scores. The tools do the math, so the odds match the simulator exactly.

      2. Every number gets checked

        Each number in an answer has to match something a tool returned, at the precision it's written. If one doesn't, the assistant gets one chance to fix it. If it still can't, you get "couldn't answer reliably" instead. Every answer has a "Show the work" list of the lookups behind it.

      3. It was tested first

        The test set has 160 questions with answers computed from the model, not written by hand, including 15 it should turn down, like injuries or betting picks, and real questions people asked here. On the 60 questions run so far it got 59 right, for about a tenth of a cent each.

      What the chat can't do

      • Know anything the model doesn't. Injuries, rosters, 2026 and betting lines are all out of scope, so it says so instead of guessing.
      • Remember numbers between questions. Follow-ups like "and at home?" work, but every number in a new answer gets looked up again.
      • Catch every wrong word. The check confirms each number came from a tool, but it can't tell if a sentence pins the right number on the wrong team.
      • Answer unlimited questions. To keep costs down, each visitor gets 10 questions an hour and the whole site has a daily budget.
